Frequently Asked Questions

Which states regulate AI in healthcare utilization review?

Enacted examples include California SB 1120, Texas SB 815, Maryland HB 820, Nebraska LB 77, Arizona HB 2175, Indiana HB 1271, Washington SB 5395 and Alabama SB 63. Our database tracks 40 states with healthcare AI legislation; the flagship table above lists each law's status and effective date.

Can AI deny health insurance claims?

Increasingly, not on its own. California requires a licensed physician or other licensed health care professional to make medical-necessity denials; Texas bars utilization review agents from using an automated decision system to make an adverse determination; Nebraska and Washington bar AI from being the sole basis for denying, delaying or modifying care, and Alabama requires a licensed physician or other qualified professional to make those decisions.

Do health plans need to disclose AI use?

In several states. Nebraska LB 77 requires utilization review agents to disclose AI use to the state, network providers, enrollees and on their website. Maryland HB 820 requires quarterly reports stating whether AI was used in adverse decisions. Alabama SB 63 requires prominent written disclosure of AI use in utilization review policies, and Indiana HB 1271 requires disclosure of AI use in adverse determinations and downcoding.
